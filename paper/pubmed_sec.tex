\subsection{Study bias (NCBI Gene, PubMed counts): it explains the curated-label depletion but not the LOEUF depletion}
Curated labels (ClinGen, HPO, ClinVar) favour well-studied genes. We counted PubMed records per human gene from NCBI Gene (gene\_info and gene2pubmed; \texttt{ncbi\_pubmed\_bias}, committed before running) and added a publication-matched control (deciles of $\log_{10}(1+n)$). The positive control passed (conserved targets are better studied, 37/1, $p=1.4\times10^{-10}$), but the mapping gate failed: 89.8\% of universe symbols matched an NCBI symbol against a 95\% bar. The pre-registered script scores unmapped symbols as zero publications, so we add a sensitivity analysis, labelled not pre-registered, that drops them (\texttt{ncbi\_pubmed\_posthoc}). Unmapped symbols are more common in CNN sets (35/8 miRNAs; median 11.0\% vs 9.2\%).

Both analyses agree. CNN candidates are less studied than every control. Against publication-matched sets the LOEUF depletion stays at 43/0, so study bias does not explain it, which is expected because LOEUF comes from population sequencing, not from the literature. The HPO depletion disappears (P5 falsified, and reversed in the post-hoc split). This explains why the curated labels (ClinGen, HPO) disagreed with LOEUF: their depletion signal is largely study bias. Because gate G2 failed, the verdicts carry that caveat; the post-hoc split is not a substitute for a pre-registered test.
