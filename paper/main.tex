\title{Does a duplex-aware CNN add to CLIP prediction after expression control? A bounded benchmark of non-conserved microRNA targets}
\date{September 2026}
\begin{document}\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Primary question and endpoint:} does the CNN improve AGO-CLIP
classification beyond seed-site counts, UTR length, and HEK293 expression?
Under gene-grouped cross-validation on 435{,}394 expression-matched
(miRNA,gene) rows, pooled AUROC rises from $0.69417$ to $0.69494$
($\Delta=+0.00077$). Expression alone reaches $0.68212$, exceeding the
site-count and CNN models without expression. This conditional estimate is
small and does not establish practical utility; a pooled-effect confidence
interval that respects both miRNA and gene dependence and an independent-library
replication remain outstanding; a cluster bootstrap of the per-miRNA median
is reported separately. The earlier
site-count/length-only result ($\Delta$AUROC $+0.0060$ on 476{,}369 rows)
is a secondary, less controlled comparator, not the headline. The model was
trained on 264{,}563 TargetScan conserved sites and scanned non-conserved seed
matches, but neither the known miR-7-5p--CDR1as positive control nor the
candidate list independently validates new targets. On literature labels,
miRDB beats our CNN (miRTarBase pooled AUROC $0.787$ vs $0.685$), and
TarBase reproduces the gap. \textbf{What this is not:} a clinical test, a
validated target-discovery assay, or evidence that a pairing-map branch is
worth its $20\times$ training cost. The resource ledger contains record
partitions rather than 317 independent studies; the tool gate remains
unverified. The locked random-weight falsifier failed its gate: untrained networks
reproduce the apparent dosage depletion. The earlier depletion remains an
observed property of the enumerated candidates, not a learned-model discovery.
A five-family-held-out transfer test also fails the locked ridge-comparison
criterion (mean Pearson $r=0.705$ for CNN vs $0.726$ for ridge+context);
there is no independent justification for the pairing-map cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Most mammalian miRNA targeting is quantified through context++ scores on conserved sites. Two gaps remain: (i) learned pairing models rarely beat engineered context features by a clear margin, and (ii) non-conserved sites --- the majority of seed matches --- are usually discarded rather than ranked. The single primary question is whether the CNN adds CLIP discrimination conditional on expression, seed-site counts, and UTR length. Other tests are secondary stress tests and cannot rescue a failed primary endpoint.
\section{Data}
TargetScan vert\_80: 264{,}563 human conserved sites with context++ labels; 25{,}132 human 3'UTR sequences; miR family table (221 broadly conserved human miRNAs, Family Conservation $=2$). ENCORI/starBase miRNATarget API (hg38, clipExpNum$\ge1$): per-miRNA CLIP-supported gene sets for 23 panel miRNAs.
\section{Methods}
\subsection{DuplexCNN architecture}
A pairing map $P\in\{0,1\}^{4\times 4\times W}$ encodes UTR window against miRNA nucleotides; site-type embedding and normalized context features $F$ (3' pairing, local AU, position, SA per Grimson et al.) join at the head:
\begin{equation}
\hat y = w^{\top}\big[\phi_{\mathrm{conv}}(P)\,\Vert\,e_{\mathrm{site}}\,\Vert\,F\big]+b,
\qquad \mathcal L=\frac1N\sum (y_i-\hat y_i)^2 .
\end{equation}
Context features are standardized on the training split only; genes (not sites) define the 80/20 split, preventing transcript leakage.
\subsection{Non-conserved scan}
For each broadly conserved miRNA we enumerate 8mer and 7mer-A1 seed matches with zero overlap to any TargetScan conserved interval, subsample to 400k, and score with the trained model.
\subsection{CLIP falsification design}
Unit of analysis: (miRNA, gene). Genes carrying a conserved site for any miRNA sharing the seed are excluded, so CLIP labels cannot be explained by conserved targeting. Features: $\min$ and $\sum$ CNN scores per gene, $\log(1+n_{8\mathrm{mer}})$, $\log(1+n_{7\mathrm{mer}})$, $\log$ UTR length, miRNA one-hots. Logistic models (base vs base+CNN) compared by 5-fold CV AUROC, bootstrap CI on $\Delta$AUROC ($B=1000$), and a within-miRNA permutation null (20 shuffles).

\subsection{Pair-map ablation (architecture honesty)}
The explicit complementarity pair-map branch is ablated under the identical split, seed, and epochs: full DuplexCNN Pearson $0.8121$ / Spearman $0.7928$ vs no-pair-map $0.8092$ / $0.7898$ ($\Delta r=+0.0029$, RMSE $-0.0005$) --- but at $333.5$s vs $16.3$s train time ($20\times$ cheaper without it). Verdict: the pair-map is a minor contributor for context++ prediction; the sequence towers carry nearly all signal. The branch is retained in the archived trained artifact to permit reproduction, but is deprecated as a default for new model training. The family-held-out test below did not earn an exception at its $20\times$ cost.
\subsection{Per-miRNA feature audit: the univariate CNN signal is site-count confounding}
To ask where the pooled $\Delta$AUROC lives, we computed univariate AUROCs per miRNA for every feature in the CLIP model (132 miRNAs, 476{,}369 pairs, \texttt{results/per\_mirna\_feature\_auroc.json}). This audit surfaced a sign-convention finding: the per-miRNA AUROC in the falsification runs is computed on the \emph{negated} min-score (\texttt{roc\_auc\_score(y, -min)}), so the raw CNN min-score is univariately \emph{anticorrelated} with CLIP labels (raw median AUROC $0.469$ vs negated $0.531$; the two are exact complements, verified per miRNA). The direction is mechanistically expected: genes with more seed sites draw more scores and hence a lower minimum, and site count itself predicts CLIP positivity --- so the univariate CNN-min signal is seed-count confounding, not pairing information. Head-to-head against the best seed feature per miRNA, the raw CNN-min wins $0/132$ (Wilcoxon $p=2.1\times10^{-23}$). The honest reading of the pooled result is therefore narrow: conditioned on site counts and UTR length the CNN adds a small complementary signal ($\Delta$AUROC $+0.0060$), but it carries no usable standalone ranking signal within a miRNA. Candidate prioritization should use the multivariate score, never the raw CNN score alone.

\subsection{Breadth audit: the conditional CNN gain is distributed, not carried by a few miRNAs}
The pooled $\Delta$AUROC of $+0.0060$ could in principle be driven by a handful of
miRNAs. We decomposed it: for each of the 131 panel miRNAs with $\ge50$ CLIP-positive
and $\ge50$ negative genes we re-fit both logistic models (covariates
$[n_8,n_7,\mathrm{len}]$ vs covariates$+$CNN $[\min,\mathrm{sum}]$) under 5-fold
cross-validation within that miRNA alone and recorded the per-miRNA
$\Delta$AUROC (\texttt{scripts/clip\_per\_mirna\_delta.py},
\texttt{results/clip\_per\_mirna\_delta.json}, three hermetic tests). The gain is
broad but small: median $+0.0046$, IQR $[-0.0013,+0.0155]$, $64.9\%$ of miRNAs
positive, Wilcoxon signed-rank $p=2.0\times10^{-9}$ across miRNAs
(Figure~\ref{fig:permirna_delta}). The largest gains are real but modest
(hsa-miR-500a-3p $+0.084$, hsa-miR-302a-3p $+0.079$, hsa-miR-93-5p $+0.072$,
hsa-miR-20a-5p $+0.070$; three of the five share the miR-17-family seed and all
five have large pair counts), and the worst cases are only mildly negative (hsa-miR-615-3p $-0.014$,
hsa-miR-652-3p $-0.014$). Two honest readings follow. First, the pooled effect
is not an artifact of one dominant miRNA: the sign test across 131 independent
miRNAs would be significant on its own. Second, the per-miRNA conditional gain
is an order of magnitude smaller than the headline pooled number for most of the
panel, so for any individual miRNA the CNN score remains a weak assistant to
site-count features, not a replacement --- consistent with the univariate audit
above.

\subsection{Expression confound: HEK293 abundance explains most of the CLIP signal (negative)}
ENCORI AGO-CLIP libraries come mostly from HEK293 cells, and a transcript that is
abundant in HEK293 yields more CLIP reads whether or not the miRNA regulates it.
We tested this directly with the Human Protein Atlas cell-line RNA atlas
(\texttt{rna\_celline.tsv.zip}, HEK293 nTPM; derived table committed as
\texttt{results/hpa\_hek293\_ntpm.tsv}). Of the 476{,}369 committed CLIP rows,
435{,}394 matched an HPA gene symbol (1{,}891 genes unmatched and dropped).
CLIP-positive genes are far more expressed: median $\log(1+\mathrm{nTPM})$ 2.79
vs 1.76 for negatives (Mann--Whitney $p$ below double-precision underflow).
Under gene-grouped 5-fold cross-validation (no gene in both train and test;
\texttt{scripts/expression\_confound.py}, \texttt{results/expression\_confound.json},
four hermetic tests) the pooled AUROCs
(Figure~\ref{fig:exprconf}) are: site counts $[n_8,n_7,\mathrm{len}]$ 0.568;
site counts$+$CNN 0.573; HEK293 expression alone 0.682;
site counts$+$expression 0.694; and
site counts$+$expression$+$CNN 0.695.
Expression alone beats every sequence model we have.
Adding expression shrinks the CNN's conditional pooled gain from $+0.0048$ to
$+0.0008$, about a six-fold reduction. Per miRNA (130 miRNAs with $\ge50$
positives and negatives, covariates now including expression) the gain survives
in sign but is tiny: median $+0.0004$, IQR $[-0.0007,+0.0029]$, $60.8\%$
positive, Wilcoxon $p=5.1\times10^{-4}$.
\emph{Primary-endpoint verdict:} the point estimate is positive but only $+0.0008$ AUROC. The per-miRNA signed-rank result does not substitute for a miRNA-clustered interval or independent-library replication; no practical-value or target-discovery pass is declared. The earlier $+0.0060$ comparison is secondary after controlling the dominant confound. Every earlier CLIP number in this paper should be read with this
correction. The obvious next control, CLIP read depth normalized by RNA-seq in the
same library, needs per-library data that ENCORI does not expose.

gene-grouped CV. HEK293 expression (HPA nTPM) alone outperforms site counts and
the CNN. Right: per-miRNA $\Delta$AUROC of adding the CNN to site counts$+$expression

\subsection{Decision rule for current and future use of the CNN score}
The expression-controlled primary comparison gives a pooled $+0.00077$
AUROC point estimate, but it has neither a joint gene-and-family
refit-aware uncertainty interval nor an independent CLIP-library replication.
The later fixed-prediction gene-cluster interval below does not fill that gap. The full CNN also failed the locked family-held-out ridge
comparison reported below. Our current rule is therefore \emph{do not
assert practical added value}: use site counts, UTR length and measured
HEK293 expression as the simpler comparator for this dataset, and do not
rank targets by raw CNN min-score alone. A future claim that the CNN adds
value requires a separately locked, externally replicated comparison on
matched library-normalized labels, a dependence-aware interval on its
conditional pooled AUROC increment, and a prospectively defined
minimum useful increment for the intended decision. No such practical
threshold has been supplied here; selecting one after seeing a $+0.00077$
point estimate would make a misleading pass. This is an operational
interpretation of limited evidence, not a new statistical gate or a
recommendation to use the comparator clinically.

\subsection{Cluster-level uncertainty on the expression-adjusted effect}
A 10{,}000-replicate percentile bootstrap resampled the 130 miRNAs as
clusters, using the stored per-miRNA expression-adjusted conditional AUROC
increments (the script and result are committed in the repository;
random seed 20260927).
The across-miRNA median is $+0.00042$, with a bootstrap 95\% interval
$[+0.00009,+0.00099]$; 79 of 130 miRNAs have positive increments. This
interval describes the median within-miRNA increment, \emph{not} the pooled
$+0.00077$ AUROC and not a confidence interval on the change for a new
individual miRNA. Related miRNAs can share seeds and genes; miRNA-level
resampling may understate dependence by family or common genes. In an
exploratory family-cluster bootstrap, the 130 miRNAs map to 103 TargetScan
human families; resampling entire families 10{,}000 times gives a 95\%
percentile interval $[+0.000023,+0.001218]$ for the same median increment
(\texttt{results/family\_cluster\_interval.json}). This accounts for
within-family ties but not shared genes across families, and is still not
an interval for the pooled $+0.00077$ AUROC. Separately, an exploratory
fixed-prediction gene-cluster bootstrap resampled 16{,}835 gene symbols,
carrying all 435{,}394 matched miRNA--gene rows with them; 300 replicates
produced a pooled-increment percentile interval of
$[+0.000332,+0.001183]$ (0 nonpositive replicates).
The original out-of-fold AUROCs were reproduced exactly; details are in
\texttt{results/pooled\_gene\_interval.json}. This conditional calculation
holds predictions fixed; it neither refits models nor accounts jointly for
family and gene dependence. The original global scaling before folds can
transmit test-distribution information into training. Thus the interval
is a sensitivity check, not a full pooled uncertainty bound or a repair
of preprocessing. None of these small positive intervals establishes
practical value or substitutes for a separate CLIP-library replication.
\subsection{Biophysical sanity check: duplex MFE vs shuffled controls}
As a sequence-level pipeline validation, the 20 top diverse candidate windows ($\pm25$ nt) were cofolded with their mature miRNA (ViennaRNA RNAcofold) against 10 mononucleotide-shuffled miRNA controls each (\texttt{results/rnafold\_validation.json}). Real duplexes are more stable than shuffled controls in $19/20$ pairs (median $z=2.33$; Wilcoxon $p<10^{-4}$); the exception, miR-7-5p$\to$REG3G ($z=-0.57$), is kept in the candidate table as a flagged row. Because candidates are selected as seed matches this is not independent biological validation --- it verifies that the exact predicted windows are sequence-correct, duplex-competent sites, closing the pipeline from UTR coordinate to physical duplex.

\subsection{Independent literature benchmark: miRTarBase 10.0}
All evaluations above use TargetScan or ENCORI labels. As an independent check we benchmarked the frozen duplex CNN against miRTarBase 10.0~\cite{mtb}: positives were strong-evidence (reporter-assay) human MTIs for the 132-miRNA panel (\texttt{miRTarBase\_SE\_R.csv}, 12{,}981 records), and negatives were genes with no human MTI of any evidence class in the full 337~MB \texttt{hsa\_MTI.csv} (10 negatives per positive, capped at 500; download integrity verified against the server Content-Length after one truncated fetch was discarded). Genes are scored by the minimum CNN score over enumerated 8mer/7mer-A1 seed windows (\texttt{results/mirtarbase\_validation.json}). On 87 miRNAs with $\geq 10$ validated positives (45 skipped), 8{,}075 rows and 2{,}164 positives, the pooled AUROC is $0.6829$; the median per-miRNA AUROC is $0.6926$ with $85/87$ miRNAs above chance (within-miRNA $z$ Mann--Whitney $p=1.0\times10^{-152}$; 1000-rep permutation $p\le 9.99\times10^{-4}$, the resolution floor). Because the CLIP audit identified seed-site count as a confound, we re-scored the identical rows with pure site count as the predictor (\texttt{results/mirtarbase\_sitecount\_control.json}): count alone reaches $0.6573$ pooled / $0.6675$ median, so most of the literature signal is seed abundance. The CNN adds a small but supported margin over count (median per-miRNA $\Delta=+0.0349$, positive for $65.5\%$ of miRNAs, Wilcoxon $p=1.2\times10^{-3}$). Two caveats bound the claim: only seed-bearing targets are scored (seed-match recall e.g. $24/34$ for let-7a-5p), and miRTarBase negatives are absence-of-record, not assayed non-targets.

\subsection{Head-to-head vs a public leader: miRDB v6.0 wins (negative)}
We compared the frozen duplex CNN against miRDB v6.0 (MirTarget)~\cite{mirdb} on the identical miRTarBase rows (\texttt{scripts/mirdb\_benchmark.py}, \texttt{results/mirdb\_benchmark.json}). The full miRDB prediction file (59~MB, 3{,}375{,}741 human rows) was downloaded; RefSeq accessions were mapped to gene symbols with the MyGene.info batch API~\cite{mygene} (29{,}460 of 29{,}509 mapped). A gene's miRDB score is the maximum over its transcripts; pairs absent from miRDB (which reports only scores $\geq 50$) score 0. On the 84 panel miRNAs present in miRDB (7{,}909 rows, 2{,}113 positives), miRDB reaches pooled AUROC $0.787$ vs $0.685$ for our CNN; median per-miRNA AUROC is $0.791$ vs $0.693$ (median $\Delta=+0.093$ in miRDB's favour), miRDB wins on $74/84$ miRNAs, Wilcoxon $p=2.4\times10^{-12}$. miRDB lists $68.9\%$ of positives but only $15.5\%$ of negatives. \emph{Verdict:} on literature-validated targets our model is clearly behind the public leader. One caveat favours miRDB: MirTarget was trained on external CLIP/expression data that may overlap literature-validated targets, so this is not a leakage-free comparison; but the size and consistency of the gap make it a real negative, and we make no state-of-the-art claim for target ranking.

\subsection{Second independent benchmark: TarBase v9.0 (negative for the CNN)}
To check that the miRTarBase result is not an artefact of one curation pipeline, we relabelled the \emph{same} 7{,}909 rows (84 miRNAs, identical gene universe and frozen CNN scores) using TarBase v9.0~\cite{tarbase}, the DIANA collection of experimentally supported interactions (4{,}724{,}537 human records scanned; \texttt{scripts/tarbase\_benchmark.py}, \texttt{results/tarbase\_benchmark.json}). A row is positive if the pair has at least one TarBase record in the evidence class. Three predictors are compared: our CNN, miRDB v6.0, and the plain seed-site count (8mer + 7mer-A1).

miRDB beats the CNN on 73/83 miRNAs for any-method labels (Wilcoxon $p=3.2\times10^{-13}$), 56/69 for low-yield labels ($p=3.0\times10^{-8}$) and 73/80 for CLIP labels ($p=2.1\times10^{-12}$). Against the seed-site count the CNN wins the pooled AUROC by less than 0.02 in every class, but \emph{loses} per miRNA: it beats the count on only 32/83 miRNAs for any-method labels ($p=0.0078$) and 32/80 for CLIP labels ($p=0.013$); for low-yield labels it wins 42/69 (median 0.654 vs 0.639, $p=0.056$, not significant). \emph{Verdict:} the second database reproduces the miRDB gap and removes the small margin over site counting that the miRTarBase control showed. Our CNN adds no reliable per-miRNA value over counting seed matches on TarBase labels.

A data-quality finding: 2{,}199 of the rows used as miRTarBase \emph{negatives} have TarBase support (2{,}063 of them from CLIP-class methods, only 7 from low-yield methods). The miRTarBase negative set is therefore contaminated by CLIP-supported interactions, which lowers every predictor's measured AUROC on that benchmark. Low-yield TarBase positives overlap miRTarBase strong-evidence positives almost fully (1{,}012 of 1{,}019), so that label class is not independent of miRTarBase; the any-method and CLIP classes are the more independent tests. Caveats: TarBase CLIP records overlap the ENCORI CLIP data used in our falsification analyses (not in CNN training), and miRDB's MirTarget was trained on CLIP and array data (leakage in its favour).

\subsection{Third arm: pure duplex thermodynamics (RNAhybrid) is near chance}
The miRDB comparison has an asymmetry: miRDB's MirTarget is a learned model with
many features, our CNN is sequence-only. We added the cleanest possible
biophysical arm: RNAhybrid 2.1.2 minimum-free-energy duplex prediction
(\texttt{-s 3utr\_human}, best hit per transcript, minimum over a gene's
transcripts; no training data, no conservation, no machine learning) on the
same committed miRTarBase rows (script
\texttt{rnahybrid\_benchmark.py}; \texttt{results/rnahybrid\_benchmark.json};
\texttt{results/rnahybrid\_rows.csv}; three hermetic tests). Rows with no RNAhybrid hit are scored one step below the
weakest observed hit. On 80 panel miRNAs (7{,}914 rows) RNAhybrid is near
chance: median per-miRNA AUROC 0.542 (pooled 0.522) with median gene coverage
$54\%$. The CNN wins 76/80 miRNAs (medians 0.693 vs 0.542, Wilcoxon
$p=2.0\times10^{-14}$). Together with the miRDB arm this gives a clean
three-way ordering on identical rows (Figure~\ref{fig:rnahybrid}): a full
machine-learning predictor (miRDB 0.791) $>$ our sequence-only CNN (0.693)
$>$ pure duplex thermodynamics (0.542). Two honest readings. First, the CNN's
margin over the strongest physics-only baseline is large, so the sequence
encoding does capture real, generalizing signal beyond hybridization energy.
Second, the equally large gap from CNN to miRDB shows that the extra signal
miRDB exploits (cross-species conservation, site context, CLIP-trained
features) is worth more than our whole model: thermodynamics is not the
bottleneck, feature breadth is.

\subsection{Fourth arm: alignment-based biophysics (miRanda) sits between}
The RNAhybrid arm isolates duplex free energy; miRanda 3.3a is the other
canonical pure-biophysics predictor class - Smith-Waterman seed-weighted
alignment plus Vienna energy, default cutoffs, no training data and no
conservation (\texttt{scripts/miranda\_benchmark.py};
\texttt{results/miranda\_benchmark.json}; \texttt{results/miranda\_rows.csv};
hermetic tests). On the identical 80-miRNA, 7{,}914-row miRTarBase benchmark,
miRanda lands strictly between the two existing arms: pooled AUROC 0.610
(median per-miRNA 0.624, median gene coverage 80\%) versus CNN 0.682
(median 0.693) and RNAhybrid 0.522. The CNN wins 66/80 miRNAs against miRanda
(Wilcoxon $p=6.0e-08$). The four-way ordering on this benchmark is now
miRDB (0.787) $>$ DuplexCNN (0.682) $>$ miRanda (0.610) $>$ RNAhybrid (0.522)
(Figure~\ref{fig:miranda}). The reading is consistent with the third arm:
adding alignment shape on top of duplex energy buys real signal
($+0.088$ pooled over RNAhybrid), but the learned sequence encoding buys
more ($+0.073$ over miRanda), and miRDB's feature breadth buys most.

\section{Documented negatives}
(1) Sequence-only DuplexCNN does not beat ridge. (2) CLIP effect size is small. (3) miR-125b-5p and miR-7-5p per-miRNA AUROCs (negated convention) are at/below chance --- reported per miRNA, not averaged away. (4) Pair-map branch contributes only $\Delta r=+0.0029$ (ablation). (5) miRDB v6.0 beats our CNN on miRTarBase rows (pooled AUROC 0.787 vs 0.685, 74/84 miRNAs, $p=2.4\times10^{-12}$). (6) On TarBase v9.0 labels the CNN loses to miRDB on 73/83 miRNAs and to the plain seed-site count per miRNA (CNN better on only 32/83, $p=0.0078$). (7) HEK293 expression alone (HPA nTPM, AUROC 0.682) beats all sequence models on CLIP labels and cuts the CNN's conditional pooled gain from $+0.0048$ to $+0.0008$. (8) g:Profiler finds no pathway coherence in CNN top-200 (13/13 miRNAs zero terms) or CLIP top-200 target sets; an expression top-200 control gives 47--115 terms. (9) STRING network density: CNN top-200 no denser than random; the CLIP+ excess over random (13/13) does not survive an expression-matched control (8 wins, 4 losses, $p=0.19$). (10) ENCODE miRNA abundance (12 lines) does not predict per-miRNA CNN AUROC ($\rho=0.076$, $p=0.195$) or gain; pre-registered miR-122/HepG2 gate failed (zero in all lines). (11) GTEx: CNN non-conserved top-200 targets show no tissue avoidance (1 win, 7 losses) and no tissue specificity (5/3, $p=0.36$); pre-registered TargetScan arm empty by construction. (12) Enrichr: HEK293 expression top-200 names the right miRNA nearly as well as the CNN (H3 falsified, 7/4); the CNN's reverse-lookup edge over site count (11/0 on the discovery panel) failed pre-registered held-out replication (16/10, $p=0.16$, 30 miRNAs). (13) Reactome audit: pre-registered gates G1 (tie-ordered top-3 rule) and G3 (40\% unmapped) failed; verdicts inconclusive, no sign of pathway coherence. (14) OmniPath non-miRTarBase labels: CNN above chance (11/2) but not above site count (8/5, $p=0.29$) and below HEK293 expression (3/10). (15) UniProt KW-0805: conserved targets enriched for transcription regulators (13/0), CNN non-conserved targets not (6/7 vs random, 4/9 vs site count). (16) gnomAD LOEUF: conserved targets constrained (13/0); CNN top-200 less constrained than every control (0/13). The reversal was found post hoc; its pre-registered held-out replication (30 miRNAs) confirmed it against uniform (30/0), expression-matched (30/0) and UTR-length-matched (25/5) sets. A later post-hoc fixed-width UTR-length-bin audit is reported below, but the random-weight falsifier failed and the trained-model claim is withdrawn. (17) ClinGen HI genes (independent label, pre-registered, 43 miRNAs): the CNN depletion holds against expression-matched sets (28/14) but not against uniform (26/16, $p=0.082$) or UTR-length-matched sets (pooled ratio 0.99). The gnomAD clause \emph{beyond UTR length} is not supported by ClinGen. (18) HPO Mendelian disease genes (pre-registered, 43 miRNAs): CNN depletion holds against uniform (30/10) and expression-matched sets (27/15) but not UTR-length-matched sets (22/21). (19) ClinVar PLP genes: pre-registered positive-control gate failed (21/15, $p=0.203$); CNN results treated as uninterpretable. (20) DepMap common essentials: no CNN depletion (E1--E3 falsified; pooled ratios above 1) and the positive-control gate failed (20/17). (21) Complex Portal subunits: CNN depletion against uniform (29/12) vanishes against expression-matched (19/23) and UTR-length-matched sets (19/24); the dosage-balance prediction fails. (22) NCBI PubMed counts: pre-registered mapping gate failed (89.8\% vs 95\%); CNN candidates are less studied than all controls, and the HPO depletion disappears against publication-matched sets (20/23), so the curated-label signal is largely study bias; the LOEUF depletion survives (43/0). (23) DECIPHER HI predictions: CNN depletion holds against expression-matched (41/2) and publication-matched sets (39/4) but not UTR-length-matched sets (25/18, $p=0.180$); the ``beyond UTR length'' clause is withdrawn. (24) Coding-length explanation of the LoF-count-label split (pre-registered): falsified; the LOEUF and $s_\mathrm{het}$ depletion survives expected-LoF matching (43/0, 42/1), and the split between labels remains unexplained. (25) BioMart paralogs: both pre-registered gates failed (coverage 89.5\%, positive control $p=0.065$); no paralog excess in CNN sets (22/20, 17/24). (26) HGNC gene families: pre-registered coverage gate failed (88.7\% vs 90\%); CNN candidates are not enriched for C2H2 zinc fingers or large families (21/22, 21/22), and the LOEUF depletion survives removal of all C2H2-ZNF genes (43/0). (27) Orphanet disease genes (pre-registered, gates passed): CNN depletion against expression-matched sets (30/13) disappears against publication-matched sets (21/21); no depletion of Orphanet loss-of-function genes against publication- (20/23) or UTR-length-matched sets (20/23). (28) MGI mouse mutant phenotypes (pre-registered, gates passed): mortality depletion holds against expression-matched sets (28/14) but not publication- (26/16, $p=0.082$) or UTR-length-matched sets (26/17, $p=0.111$); embryo-phenotype depletion against publication-matched sets (29/14, $p=0.016$) would not survive a Bonferroni correction.
\subsection{Locked random-weight and score-permutation falsifier}
The dosage-depletion interpretation faced an independently locked test, separate
from the primary expression-controlled CLIP endpoint. We used 200{,}000
seed-0-subsampled non-conserved candidates, 43-panel miRNAs, the top 200 per
miRNA, and three expected-LoF-matched comparison draws. The trained network
showed depletion in 31 panels by LOEUF (L1) and 33 by $s_\mathrm{het}$ (L2).
Under 100 independently initialized, \emph{untrained} DuplexCNNs, the
99th-percentile panel counts were 33 for L1 and 33 for L2. In a separate
100-draw within-miRNA permutation of the trained scores, the corresponding
99th percentiles were 22 and 22.01. Both score-permutation comparisons favour
the trained model; neither trained count strictly exceeds the random-weight
99th percentile. The locked rule required beating \emph{both} nulls at
$\alpha=0.01$ for each claim. Thus both L1 and L2 fail. The result does not
prove that the precise physical mechanism is site enumeration alone, but it
does show that trained weights are unnecessary to reach the observed level of
depletion under this pipeline. We withdraw the claim that this depletion is a
new biological finding learned by DuplexCNN; the older observed comparisons
remain descriptive and cannot rescue this gate. Full 100+100 arrays, input
hashes, code and verdict are in
\texttt{results/random\_weight\_falsify.json} and
\texttt{results/recovered\_inputs\_20260927.json}. The fresh TargetScan
extraction and deterministic weight rebuild reproduce the archived training
metric triple exactly; original ignored-input byte hashes were unavailable
after a workspace loss, so byte identity with that earlier environment cannot
be claimed. This negative result does not alter the separately specified
CLIP primary endpoint; its remaining external replication and dependence-aware
interval are still open.

\subsection{Family-held-out transfer test and the pair-map decision}
Before inspecting its outcome, we locked a transfer test over the five largest
miRNA families in the deterministic 80{,}000-site TargetScan context++ build.
For each family, we retrained the full DuplexCNN with context for six epochs on
all other families, fit ridge+context ($\alpha=10$) on the same training sites,
and scored Pearson correlation on the held-out family. A fresh untrained
DuplexCNN provided a random-weight comparison. Context normalization was
fit on training families only. The locked rule required the CNN's mean Pearson
$r$ across the five held-out families to exceed ridge+context \emph{and} to
exceed the random-weight mean by more than $0.05$.

miR-15-5p group & 4{,}143 & 0.7425 & 0.7949 & -0.0231\\
miR-302-3p group & 4{,}094 & 0.7127 & 0.7249 & -0.1533\\
miR-17-5p group & 4{,}029 & 0.7553 & 0.7396 & -0.1517\\

\hline

\end{tabular}

The CNN beat ridge in only one of five families. Its unweighted mean exceeded
the random-weight mean by $0.8159$, passing that clause, but trailed ridge by
$0.0214$, failing the other. The joint locked verdict is \emph{no support for
transferable advantage over ridge}. This is TargetScan context++ transfer across families, not external CLIP
validation. The test compares the \emph{full} CNN with ridge, so it cannot
isolate the pair-map branch. Its within-dataset gain was only $+0.0029$
Pearson $r$ at $20\times$ training cost. We therefore deprecate the branch
for new training without changing the archived artifact. Exact family outcomes:
\texttt{results/family\_transfer.json}; locked code:
\texttt{scripts/family\_transfer.py}.

\subsection{Fixed-width absolute UTR-length-bin sensitivity audit}
A coarse UTR-length-decile control in the earlier 30-miRNA held-out panel
left a median-LOEUF contrast in 25 of 30 miRNAs. After the random-weight
falsifier failed, we tested finer \emph{absolute} UTR-length bins as a
post-hoc sensitivity audit, not as a way to restore the trained-model claim.
For each miRNA we selected the original 200 CNN-ranked candidates among
LOEUF-mapped genes. A candidate was matchable only when its absolute length
bin contained at least one non-candidate from the same miRNA universe;
unmatchable candidates were excluded from both candidate and comparison
medians. Three seeded control samples drew one gene per matchable candidate,
with replacement inside its bin. Duplicate controls therefore reduce the
effective comparison-set size, and there was no nearest-bin fallback.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
Absolute bin width & Matchable/6{,}000 & Median unique controls & Panels $\uparrow$/\,$\downarrow$ & Descriptive $p$\\
\hline
25 nt & 5{,}803 & 174 & 21/9 & 0.0214\\
50 nt & 5{,}884 & 175 & 24/6 & 0.00072\\
100 nt & 5{,}948 & 177 & 23/7 & 0.00261\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
An upward arrow means higher median LOEUF (less constraint) among the
\emph{matchable subset} of CNN-selected genes than the average matched
control median. The $p$ values are unadjusted one-sided sign tests, not
pre-registered gates; the 25/50/100-nt audits reuse the same panel and
cannot be counted as three independent replications. The loss of up to 197
candidates at 25 nt and sampling with replacement limit the interpretation.
The result describes how a selected-gene pattern responds to finer length
matching; because untrained networks matched the earlier depletion under the
locked falsifier, it supplies no evidence that learned weights produced the
pattern. The exact per-miRNA exclusions and medians are in the result JSON files
for each width (\texttt{results/exact\_length\_bin\_*bp.json}); the
seeded drawing code is in \texttt{scripts/exact\_length\_bin\_control.py}.
